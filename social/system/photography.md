# Photography

v0: Claude writes the search terms, Angela downloads from Unsplash, and Claude picks. The goal is photos that feel like ours: real sellers, real places, natural light, a little cinematic. Never "stock".

## Who and what we photograph

Our audience is Amazon and Walmart sellers, resellers, brand owners, ecommerce operators and their teams. Picture them: someone running a business from a spare room, a garage stacked with boxes, a small warehouse, a kitchen table, a car outside a store, a train on the way to work. They're 25–60, varied, practical, busy and good at what they do.

Good subjects:
- hands on things: taping a box, scanning a barcode, holding a phone in a store aisle, a laptop on a cluttered desk;
- people mid-task, absorbed, side-on or looking at a phone or screen, not at the camera;
- places of work: shelving, cardboard, a packing table, a delivery van, a retail aisle, a home office at night;
- quiet moments: coffee before the day, a phone on a train, someone walking out of a store with a bag.

## Writing search terms

Unsplash search is literal. Generic words return generic stock. Good terms name **a person, an action, a place and a light**.

| Weak (returns stock) | Strong |
|---|---|
| business woman laptop | woman packing boxes garage evening light |
| ecommerce | man taping cardboard box warehouse window |
| happy team office | two people looking at phone train platform |
| online shopping | hands scanning barcode store shelf |
| success | small business owner coffee morning kitchen table |

Rules:
- 3–6 words. Concrete nouns and verbs. Add one light or mood word: *morning, window light, evening, golden hour, overcast*.
- Write 4–6 terms per post from different angles: the person, the hands, the place, the moment.
- Add "film" or "35mm" to one term to pull more cinematic results.
- Never search for abstract ideas (growth, profit, AI, technology). Search for what that idea looks like in a seller's day.
- For a post about AI Operator, search for the human moment the prompt saves: the person in the aisle, the phone in the hand. Never robots, glowing brains or code on screens.

Tell Angela, per post:
- the terms;
- **look for:** e.g. "portrait orientation, the person on one side with space on the other, darker bottom third";
- **skip:** anything posed, smiling at the camera, white-studio, visible brand logos or competitor products;
- **how many:** 6–10 photos per post, portrait orientation where possible (it crops to square, 4:5 and 9:16), at least 2400 px on the long side (download "Original" or "Large").

## Judging photos (Claude, from the contact sheets)

**Pass** a photo only if all of these hold:
1. **Believable:** it could be a real seller's day. Nobody posing, nobody grinning at the lens, no handshake, no pointing at a laptop, no headset call-centre smile.
2. **Light:** natural or motivated light, real shadows. No flat white studio, no blue corporate grade, no HDR glow.
3. **Specific:** you can say what's happening in one sentence ("a woman checks a price on her phone in a store aisle").
4. **Room for text:** a calm, darker area in the lower third or on one side, away from faces.
5. **Crops well:** the subject survives square, 4:5 and 9:16. Check the sheet's "too small for" notes.
6. **Clean:** no visible brand names, competitor products, readable personal data or watermarks.
7. **Not AI-looking:** no waxy skin, impossible hands, garbled text or uncanny symmetry. Unsplash has some AI images; reject them.
8. **Fits the set:** across a week, mix people, hands and places, and don't repeat a location or model.

Write one line per photo you pick or reject ("#7 pass: hands, warm window light, space top right" / "#3 reject: posed smile at camera"). If nothing passes for a post, say so and write new terms. Don't settle.

Choosing `focus` (CSS background-position, e.g. `"60% 35%"`): put the subject's eyes in the upper half, and keep the lower third darker for text. Re-render and check every format.

## Licence

Unsplash photos are free for commercial use without attribution, but Unsplash+ photos need the paid licence: check the account before using them. Photos of people are fine for social as long as they don't imply the person endorses Threecolts. Never write a quote or a name onto a stranger's photo as if they said it.

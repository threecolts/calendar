# Brand strategy: how Threecolts shows up

From the Threecolts Brand Strategy deck (Nora, November 2026). Where this and `brand.md` disagree on tone or personality, this wins; product names still follow `brand.md`.

## The challenge and the insight

- **Brand challenge:** shift online sellers from seeing us as a patchwork of tools to choosing us as the one trusted ecommerce ecosystem that supports them at every stage of their selling journey.
- **Customer insight:** "The rising cost and complexity of selling across marketplaces is making it harder to run efficiently and scale profitably."
- **Who we talk to:** online sellers ready to scale. Brands (1P enterprise brands, 3P marketplace brand sellers) and resellers (1P distributors and wholesalers, 3P sellers).
- **What they feel:** money taken and owed (deductions for 1P, fees for 3P, unclaimed reimbursements), and time lost across portals, spreadsheets and disconnected tools.

## Four personality traits

| Trait | We are | We are never |
|---|---|---|
| **In your corner** | A friend who gets it. We know the selling struggle and we're here to support. | Salesy or preachy. We never push a pitch when someone needs help, or lecture sellers on how to run their business. |
| **Calm in the storm** | Steady and clear-headed. When marketplaces shift or margins get squeezed, we give perspective and a plan. | Frantic or dramatic. We never sell with fear or add noise to a chaotic day. |
| **Quietly expert** | Deep know-how in plain English, backed by hard proof, not buzzwords. | Jargon-heavy or condescending. We never overcomplicate things to prove we know our stuff. |
| **Boldly playful** | Confident, upbeat and quick with a smile. We take the work seriously, not ourselves. | Snarky or gimmicky. Our fun never tips over the top. |

## The traits as visuals (deck slide 32)

**We look like:**
- A real person, framed close enough to read their face, with a genuine, easy smile or a clear expression of focus, relief or confidence. Warm, natural light. A friend, not a model.
- Energy and lift: people mid-movement or mid-moment, upbeat without being staged.
- One plain, confident line of type with a bit of wit ("Less tab hopping. More profit popping."). Plenty of space, one idea.

**We never look like:**
- Busy, explain-everything infographics: icons, arrows, dashboards and badges crammed together, cartoon or AI-illustrated people. That is jargon-heavy and gimmicky in visual form.
- Fear: alarm colours, warning symbols, dramatic stress poses, chaos used to sell.
- Smug, snarky or meme-ish humour.

## What this means for every post

- Lead with the person and their moment, then the line. The reader should feel "they get me" before they read the product name.
- Copy is warm, plain and specific: a friend explaining, not a brand pitching. Use humour only as a light, confident touch.
- Proof over claims: a real number, result or capability from the brief, said simply.
- Products use the endorsed form when a brief asks for it, e.g. "UniCon by Threecolts", so trust in Threecolts builds with every product. Spell the product name as in `brand.md`.
